Molecular Biology - Part 2: Transcription and Transposition

Description

This course explores DNA-to-RNA transcription, mechanisms of transposition, and the experimental approaches used to investigate these processes. It develops skills in comparing prokaryotic and eukaryotic transcription, analyzing protein structures, designing experiments, and interpreting molecular biology data.

Criteria

In order to earn this credential, the learner:

  • Compared the molecular mechanisms of bacterial and eukaryotic transcription, including initiation, elongation, proofreading, regulation, and termination.
  • Designed experiments to measure transcriptional activity, map promoters and transcription start sites, distinguish stages of transcription, and test mechanistic hypotheses.
  • Selected and applied molecular biology assays—including FRET, SELEX, DNase-seq, 3C, Hi-C, ChIA-PET, GRO-seq, ChIP-seq, and MNase-seq—to investigate transcription and chromatin regulation.
  • Interpreted experimental data to infer RNA polymerase activity, promoter properties, chromatin accessibility, nucleosome positioning, histone modification, and long-range regulatory interactions.
  • Compared self-splicing introns and spliceosome-mediated pre-mRNA splicing and identified the nucleophiles, intermediates, and products involved in splicing chemistry.
  • Explained how enhancers, insulators, locus control regions, nucleosomes, histone modifications, polyadenylation, and transcription factors regulate eukaryotic gene expression.
  • Compared major mechanisms of transposition and interpreted drug-resistance colony assays, genomic Southern blots, and inside-out PCR experiments.

Innovations in Textile Engineering: Fibers, Yarns, Nonwovens, & More

Description

This course introduces the physical and engineering principles underlying fibers and textiles, including polymer science, transport behavior, manufacturing and testing methods, color engineering, and composite fibrous materials. It also explores hierarchical and bio-inspired design, sustainability, textile innovation, and the development of utility patent applications.

Criteria

In order to earn this credential, the learner:

  • Applied engineering and manufacturing principles across the textile hierarchy, from polymers and fibers to yarns, fabrics, and finished products.
  • Analyzed polymer properties and compared wet, dry, dry-wet, melt, gel, and thermal-drawing methods for manufacturing fibers.
  • Distinguished woven, knitted, and nonwoven structures and related their construction and bonding methods to functional and mechanical performance.
  • Evaluated fiber and textile properties using tensile testing, moisture and contact-angle measurements, microscopy, spectroscopy, and thermal-analysis concepts.
  • Engineered functional and smart textile concepts involving conductive fibers, thermal regulation, phase-change materials, sensors, composites, and bio-inspired design.
  • Assessed textile lifecycle impacts, resource competition, microfiber release, recyclability, supply-chain compatibility, and strategies for more sustainable manufacturing.
  • Designed a feasible textile product or technology concept and communicated it in the form of a mock utility patent application.
